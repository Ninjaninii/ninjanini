## Context

<b>The NCCU Creative Ideas Hackathon</b> (政大創意點子黑客松) is a student competition run at National Chengchi University in Taipei. Our team of three answered a brief on second careers for older workers and won the Innovation Star Award.

<p class="jx-topic"><small>Our question</small>In a super-aged society, how might we create second careers that suit what older people are actually good at?</p>

## The problem

**Experience that flows one way, and is lost.**

Older workers are mostly concentrated in manual jobs their bodies no longer suit, yet employers name experience and networks as their main strength. Meanwhile, local history and life stories are recorded one way, by experts and creators, and are hard to put to use.

## The idea

**Book a retired person's experience like a consultation.** Wisely is an online platform where older people become paid consultants for oral history, local knowledge and working experience, and anyone who needs that knowledge can search, filter and book them.

<figure class="fig ws">
<span class="fig__t">User flow</span>

<span class="fig__src">Enter → browse consultants → found one? If not, filter again → book and describe the need → consultant accepts? If not, choose another → matched → hold the session → completed? If not, reschedule → leave feedback.</span>
</figure>

## The business model

**Every booking pays for the platform.**

<figure class="fig ws">
<span class="fig__t">Business Model Canvas</span>
<span class="fig__s">From the team's final deck, in the original Mandarin</span>
<img class="fig__img" src="/work/wisely/slide-10.webp" alt="The Business Model Canvas I wrote, nine blocks from key partners to revenue streams" width="1800" height="967" loading="lazy" />
<span class="fig__src">Value: pass experience on, so older people's real value is used and generations succeed together. Customers: history lovers and travellers, researchers and students, industry researchers and founders, with people aged 45+ as consultants. Revenue: a 20–30% commission on each hour of consultation (the main stream), ads and brand partnerships, and, in the long term, anonymised data and insight licensing. Costs: salaries and platform upkeep (fixed); consultant fees and events (variable).</span>
</figure>

## The numbers

**Break-even in year six.** Year one assumes 6,000 bookings at a NT$200 platform cut: NT$1.2M in revenue. With consultants paid about NT$400 a booking and fixed costs near NT$1.5M a year, the platform breaks even at about 18,000 bookings a year.
